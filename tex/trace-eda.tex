\chapter{Demand Adequacy: Request Rate as a Proxy for Vision Demand}
\label{cap:trace-eda}
\chaptermark{Demand adequacy}

This chapter answers RQ1: how strongly does request rate misrepresent
vision-encoder demand, and what forecastable structure remains? The evidence is
an exploratory characterization of Microsoft's
\textit{AzureLMMInferenceDataset2025}~\cite{AzureLMM2025}, a production trace of
a multimodal-model inference cluster released with the ModServe
paper~\cite{Qiu2025ModServe} and the workload used throughout the thesis; all
four of its schema fields are used. Analyses run GPU-accelerated preprocessing
and model kernels (cuDF, cuML, CuPy; RTX~5090), with reduced statistics and
plotting on CPU; every figure and number is reproducible from versioned scripts
inventoried in Appendix~\ref{ap:artifacts}.

\section{Evaluation contract}
\label{sec:eda-contract}
\label{sec:eda-globals}

\begin{description}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item[Population.] The seven-day trace (2024-10-15 to 2024-10-22, UTC),
\num{1000000} requests, and its four demand series at one-minute bins (the
provisioning cadence), \num{10080} bins in all; binning sensitivity is swept
over \SI{1}{\second}--\SI{1}{\hour}.
\item[Independent unit.] The burst \emph{episode} (18 in the week) and the
\emph{day} (7). Minute bins are the measurement grid, not replicates, and
requests are never the unit of a burst claim.
\item[Endpoints.] Primary: the share of vision demand that request rate
accounts for, as the through-origin constant-fraction $R^2$ of image rate on request
rate. Secondary: burst onset time, the budget a controller has.
\item[Comparator.] Request rate, the signal a conventional autoscaler observes,
against the image rate that drives encoder work, with the decode series as the
within-trace control for an adequate proxy.
\item[Adequacy criterion.] Request rate would suffice if the constant-fraction
model reproduced the image series and the two signals' extreme minutes
coincided; forecastable structure remains if image-signal memory outlasts
replica startup (tens of seconds to minutes).
\item[Denominators and scope.] Request shares are over all one million requests
unless the sentence says \emph{image-bearing} (\num{500000}); image-volume
shares over the trace's \num{14332170} images; rate percentiles over the
\num{10080} minute bins. Global reference percentiles for all seven signals are
tabulated in the provenance manifest (Appendix~\ref{ap:artifacts});
load-bearing values are quoted inline. ``Heavy-tailed'' means large upper-tail
mass relative to the median. Every result describes \emph{this} trace
(Section~\ref{sec:eda-limitations}).
\end{description}

\section{Dataset}
\label{sec:eda-dataset}

Each request carries an arrival timestamp (millisecond precision), an image
count, a context-token count, and a generated-token count. Coverage is
continuous (largest gap \SI{39}{\second}; median inter-arrival time
\SI{312}{\milli\second}), and the 151 fully duplicated rows
($1.5\times10^{-4}$) are \emph{retained} as indistinguishable from legitimate
simultaneous arrivals, ties processed in file order. Exactly 50.0\% of requests
are text-only, a by-construction split that must be stated when generalizing
from this trace. Image counts are almost always perfect squares: 71 distinct
$k^2$ values cover 99.9894\% of image-bearing requests, up to
$97^2 = \num{9409}$ images in a single request. The 53 non-square requests
(22 distinct counts) form an explicit residual class in the request taxonomy.
The $k\times k$ structure marks tile or frame grids, but the two arrival views
differ: 66.0\% of image-bearing requests carry exactly one image, and it is
image \emph{volume}, not image-bearing request arrivals, that the bulk grids
dominate. The context-token field \emph{includes} the traced model's vision
tokens with a non-constant per-image increment, so no clean additive
decomposition into text
and vision tokens exists, as the dataset's official schema documentation
confirms (Figure~\ref{fig:eda-ctx-vs-img}).

\section{Request anatomy}
\label{sec:eda-requests}

All three request-level marginals carry large upper-tail mass relative to their
medians; generated tokens are the heaviest-tailed (Hill estimate, indicative),
so decode-side capacity is quantile- rather than mean-driven. Multimodal
requests have longer inputs but \emph{shorter} outputs than text-only requests
(context median 1{,}422 vs.\ 792 tokens; generation 80 vs.\ 147), a
question-answering profile against longer-form text chat.

Clustering all requests on $\log(1{+}x)$ for image, context and
generated-token counts renders four interpretable archetypes
(Table~\ref{tab:eda-archetypes}): $k$-means with $k{=}4$, silhouette-selected on
a near-tie, so the groups are interpretive rather than sharply distinct. Each
stresses a different serving stage: video jobs the vision encoder and prefill,
conversational text the decode stage, extraction-style requests almost nothing.
The video/frame archetype is the clustering's rendering of the
threshold-defined \emph{frames class} used elsewhere ($k{>}10$ grids: 2.7\% of
requests, 81.1\% of image volume), the two overlapping but not identical.

\section{Temporal structure}
\label{sec:eda-temporal}

The arrival process is not Poisson at any tested scale
(\SI{1}{\second}--\SI{1}{\hour}): inter-arrival coefficient of variation 1.55,
per-second counts overdispersed against Poisson$(1.65)$, and a Fano factor that
keeps growing across the measured range of counting windows, from 1.8 at
\SI{1}{\second} to 938 at one hour. Every tested aggregation scale an
autoscaler would plausibly use is therefore overdispersed. Spectrally, a diurnal harmonic family
(\SI{24}{\hour} fundamental, weaker 12/8/\SI{6}{\hour} overtones) sits on a
strong $1/f^{\beta}$ long-range-dependent background; no sharp non-harmonic
line dominates the sub-diurnal band above it.

Table~\ref{tab:eda-persistence} summarizes the four demand signals at one-minute
resolution. The load-bearing contrast is between the two pools' arrival signals:
the image signal is an order of magnitude burstier than the request
signal (CV$^2$ 3.83 vs.\ 0.32 on the same one-minute clock), further above its
own mean at peak ($15.6\times$ vs.\ $4.7\times$), longer-memoried
(autocorrelation half-life 69 vs.\ \SI{9}{\minute}) and more persistent
spectrally ($H \approx 0.93$ vs.\ $0.83$). Burstiness and forecastable structure
concentrate in the same, vision-borne signal. The picture is not monotone across
all four: the decode series pairs the \emph{shortest} half-life
(\SI{5}{\minute}) with the second-highest persistence ($H \approx 0.91$), an
arrival-stamping artifact ($\dagger$). Half-life and Hurst orderings disagree,
so no joint claim is made on them; the thesis builds only on the
images-versus-requests contrast, which every column supports.

\section{The vision signal: decoupled, bursty, single-class}
\label{sec:eda-vision}

A constant-fraction model (vision work as a fixed multiple of request rate)
explains only $R^2 = \num{0.123}$ of the image-rate series ($\alpha = 15.1$
images per request, a forced through-origin fit;
Figure~\ref{fig:eda-decouple}), while decode load is largely carried by request
rate ($R^2 = \num{0.654}$). Request rate thus carries most of the information
about decode demand and almost none about vision demand. What a policy can do
with that gap depends on system parameters (cold-start time, per-replica
capacity, cost) outside this dataset;
the trace establishes the information asymmetry itself.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{figuras/eda/eda_d_img_vs_req_scatter}
\caption[Image rate against request rate]{Image rate against request rate over
one-minute windows, with the best-fit constant-fraction line. Read vertically:
one request rate coincides with image rates spanning two orders of magnitude,
and the highest image-rate windows are not the highest request-rate windows.}
\label{fig:eda-decouple}
\end{figure}

The image-rate signal exceeds its 99th percentile (\num{224}~images/s) in 101
one-minute windows that merge into 18 burst episodes once each run is extended
to its surrounding median crossings; the six largest, plus the calmest day, form
the workload windows of Chapter~\ref{cap:closedloop}
(Figure~\ref{fig:eda-windows}). The largest episode peaks at \num{370}~images/s,
$15.6\times$ the mean image rate; the request rate at that same minute,
\num{4.48} requests/s, does sit above its own p99 (\num{4.35}) but at only
$2.7\times$ its mean, an excursion far smaller than the image signal's and one
of just five minutes in the week where the two signals' top percentiles
coincide (Figure~\ref{fig:eda-sevenday}; Section~\ref{sec:eda-multires}).
Composition is homogeneous: the frames class supplies 92--96\% of every top
episode's image volume, and clustering the 18 episodes on shape and composition
isolates a single mixed-composition outlier against an otherwise uniform
population of frames-class floods.

Bursts compound on the prefill side: multimodal requests carry 72.5\% of all
prefill tokens, and burst-minute mean prefill length is $2.69\times$ that of
calm minutes, so more images and longer prefills arrive together (window-level
images--context correlation $r=0.89$). Decode moves the other way: the
generated-token rate rises only $1.78\times$ across the same bursts and
per-request generation \emph{shortens} (median 84 vs.\ 99 tokens), so the
decode pool is nearly burst-neutral while the vision and prefill stages are not.
Onsets are fast: baseline-to-peak in a median of \SI{6.5}{\minute}, with a third
of the 18 episodes (6) peaking within one to two minutes, comparable to or faster
than replica cold-starts of tens of seconds to minutes
(Figure~\ref{fig:eda-onsets}).

\section{Signal predictability}
\label{sec:eda-predictability}

The image signal's 69-minute autocorrelation half-life pairs with
$H \approx 0.93$, a proxy derived from the $1/f^{\beta}$ spectral slope over the
sub-diurnal band (10 minutes to 12 hours). That band clears the diurnal
fundamental but still contains its 12/8/\SI{6}{\hour} harmonics, which are not
notched out, and the estimate is endpoint-sensitive: refitting with the band's
upper period at 6 hours gives $H \approx 0.90$, at 3 hours $H \approx 0.82$. The
value is indicative, not an independent Hurst estimate; the robust persistence
evidence is the autocorrelation contrast itself, 69 versus 9 minutes, with both
signals carrying the same diurnal component. Long memory suggests that purely
short-memory models (Poisson or low-order ARMA) may structurally underfit the
image signal: a risk, not a theorem, which Chapter~\ref{cap:predictors} tests
empirically.

The demand signals are not derivable from one another under any of the
relationships tested here: constant-fraction scaling, rank correlation, and
lagged linear cross-correlation. (Nonlinear or multivariate predictors with
calendar features are not ruled out by these tests;
Chapter~\ref{cap:predictors}'s covariate experiments probe that direction and
find nearly nothing to exploit at provisioning cadence.) Image count and
generation length are weakly rank-correlated at request level (Spearman
$-0.06$, with no monotonic trend in per-bucket median generation), and at
window level their
cross-correlation peaks sharply at lag zero with no lead. Below the one-minute
scale it is the image signal that loses forecastability: at
one-second cadence its best point forecast reaches 98.8\% WAPE, essentially the
zero-prediction floor of a white shot-noise signal, while the smoother signals
retain measurable skill (best point WAPE 62.9\% for requests, 81.6\% for
generated tokens, 86.0\% for context tokens; Chapter~\ref{cap:predictors}). At
that floor finer-grained inputs add noise rather than information.

\section{Resolution sensitivity and tail regimes}
\label{sec:eda-multires}

Burstiness depends on the observation window, differently per signal (per-window
peak attenuation is tabulated in the provenance manifest,
Appendix~\ref{ap:artifacts}). Coarsening hides the vision peaks far faster than
the request peaks: at \SI{60}{\second} a monitor still sees 37\% of the true
one-second request peak but only \textbf{3.9\%} of the one-second image peak, so
a minute-resolution monitor understates the encoder pool's instantaneous surges
by roughly $25\times$ and the request rate by under $3\times$. The image
signal's excess dispersion also remains the highest of the four signals at every
tested window, although it falls $6.7\times$ from \SI{1}{\second} to
\SI{60}{\second} (CV$^2$ $25.7\!\rightarrow\!3.83$;
Figure~\ref{fig:eda-multires}), consistent with its long-range dependence: no
tested aggregation window (\SI{1}{\second}--\SI{1}{\hour}) recovers a
well-behaved image signal.

\caption[Sensitivity to the observation window]{Sensitivity to the observation

The top-1\% context-token requests are 81.6\% multimodal with a median of 529
images, so the large-context tail \emph{is} the frames class rather than long
text prompts. The top-1\% image-count requests are entirely multimodal, carry
${\approx}\num{36000}$ context tokens at the median and decode briefly (53
generated tokens). The busiest 1\% of request-rate minutes, by contrast, carry
ordinary-composition traffic: median 1 image, near-global-median context and
generation. High request rate does not imply heavy per-request work. Of 106
hot-request and 101 hot-image minutes (each signal's minutes at or above its own
p99), only \textbf{5} are shared (Jaccard 0.02): the two pools' extreme-load
events are essentially disjoint. Provisioning the vision pool from the request
signal therefore misses precisely the moments that matter
(Section~\ref{sec:eda-vision}).

\section{Limitations and scope}
\label{sec:eda-limitations}

The trace carries no image resolutions (counts only), no session or user
identifiers, and no latency or completion-time ground truth; it is arrival-side
only. Its seven-day window supports diurnal but not weekly periodicity claims,
the 50/50 modality split is by construction, and the
context-token field embeds the \emph{traced} model's vision tokenization, so
token counts are self-consistent as a workload but not transferable to another
model's tokenizer.

Everything here describes \emph{this} trace, one week of one production cluster,
and the thesis's generality argument runs through mechanism, not sampling. The
near-pure frames-class composition of every major burst points to a specific
mechanism: a few tenants submitting batch video/frame pipelines into a cluster
that also serves interactive traffic. That is consistent with the bursty,
heterogeneous image traffic ModServe reports across its
fleet~\cite{Qiu2025ModServe}. Under that mechanism the decoupling should recur
wherever batch visual workloads share a serving system with interactive chat,
while its \emph{magnitude} remains workload-specific. The claim is falsifiable:
a multimodal trace in which image volume tracks request rate (uniformly
image-light requests, or admission control that spreads frames jobs) would
collapse the decoupling, and with it the case for a separate vision-pool signal.

\section{Answer to RQ1}
\label{sec:eda-answer}

On this trace request rate misrepresents vision-encoder demand severely: it
accounts for $R^2 = \num{0.123}$ of the image-rate series against \num{0.654} of
the decode series, the two signals' extreme minutes are essentially disjoint,
and minute-resolution monitoring erases most of the image peak. Forecastable
structure nevertheless survives, concentrated in that same vision-borne signal:
an autocorrelation half-life of \SI{69}{\minute} against \SI{9}{\minute} for
requests, over a burst population of 18 episodes whose median onset,
\SI{6.5}{\minute}, is short enough that reacting only after observing an onset
competes directly with replica startup. Whether that structure converts into
usable forecasts at replica-startup horizons is RQ2
(Chapter~\ref{cap:predictors}).
