\section[The ai-dynamo ecosystem]{The ai-dynamo ecosystem: the substrate this
work builds on}
\label{app:ecosystem}

NVIDIA's \texttt{ai-dynamo} project is a family of five first-party
repositories implementing datacenter-scale \emph{disaggregated}
large-language-model inference, one per layer of the deployment lifecycle. This
appendix records what those components are, how they compose, and --- the reason
it belongs here --- which of them this work consumes and which it contributes
back to.

\paragraph{aiconfigurator (offline planner).} An offline search that chooses
prefill/decode worker counts and parallelism to maximize throughput under
TTFT/TPOT SLOs, emitting ready-to-deploy Dynamo configs from a performance
database of kernel latencies \emph{collected on real silicon}. This thesis
consumes its per-operator cost model.

\paragraph{grove (Kubernetes operator).} A Go operator that deploys an entire
disaggregated topology from a single declarative Custom Resource, gang-scheduling
prefill/decode as an all-or-nothing unit. Outside this study's scope.

\paragraph{dynamo (serving hub).} The hub: an orchestration runtime that sits
\emph{above} the inference engines (vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM) rather than
replacing them, providing disaggregated prefill/decode pools, KV-aware routing
and SLA-driven autoscaling planners. Its \texttt{dynamo.replay} runtime drives
this thesis's queue replays; its \texttt{mocker} offers GPU-free simulation
reusing the aiconfigurator cost core.

\paragraph{nixl (KV-cache data plane).} A point-to-point transfer library that
moves KV cache between prefill and decode workers over RDMA. Not exercised
directly here.

\paragraph{aiperf (benchmark client).} The measurement client (successor to
GenAI-Perf): an asynchronous, multi-process tool that drives an
OpenAI-compatible endpoint and reports client-observed TTFT, inter-token latency
(ITL~$\approx$~TPOT), throughput and goodput. This thesis uses it as the live
client.

Beneath these five sit the inference engines (vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM) that
execute the forward passes; the \texttt{ai-dynamo} layer sizes, orchestrates,
transports and measures around them.

\paragraph{How they compose.} The five form one loop with observed latencies fed
back against aiconfigurator's targets. Table~\ref{tab:app-ecosystem} names each
layer and how this thesis uses it.

\paragraph{Maturity and pinning.} The stack is young and fast-moving, minor
versions landing roughly monthly, so every result targets a deliberately pinned
snapshot: \texttt{ai-dynamo} 1.2.1 (wheel) / 1.3.0 (source),
\texttt{aiconfigurator} 0.10.0 (editable, data branch) and \texttt{aiperf}
0.11.0 (Table~\ref{tab:ap-env}). Contributions are accordingly described at the
level of mechanism --- per-pool forecasting, capacity-rating sizing --- which
outlives specific APIs.

\subsection{Where this thesis fits}

This work is a provisioning-and-planning layer on the stack above, not a
modification of its serving core.

\paragraph{What it consumes.} \textbf{aiconfigurator}'s cost model prices the two
pools (Chapter~\ref{cap:twopool}), the \textbf{\texttt{dynamo.replay}} runtime
runs the queue replays, and \textbf{aiperf} drives a real Dynamo control plane as
live client (\texttt{live\_fc} / \texttt{live\_re}, Chapter~\ref{cap:validation})
--- as the table's ``Use in this thesis'' column records.

\paragraph{What it contributes back.} Two things return upstream to
\textbf{aiconfigurator}: a draft coverage pull request (the missing
patch-embedding operator, large-batch GEMM and attention coverage for vision
transformers, and a collector out-of-memory fix), and a silicon validation of
its encoder cost model against direct RTX~5090 measurements of vLLM's own vision
tower, reported in Appendix~\ref{app:engine-sensitivity}.

\paragraph{How the contribution is positioned.} The predictive per-pool
provisioning layer this thesis proposes sits \emph{above} the engines, in the
same architectural slot as Dynamo's own SLA planner, but differs in two ways: it
is \emph{forecast-driven} (it sizes ahead of demand from a per-pool time-series
forecast, rather than reacting to observed load) and \emph{modality-decoupled}
(it provisions the encoder and language pools separately, rather than on
aggregate token load). It is complementary to the stack, not a replacement for
any component of it.

\paragraph{Scope.} The study is deliberately narrow where the stack is broad: it
reasons about single-node pool economics, not multi-node \textbf{grove}
deployment; and although the live closed loop drives the real Dynamo control
plane, its engines are \emph{mocked} (TP2) --- a full-serving-path run with real
GPU \emph{engines} behind \textbf{aiperf} remains future work
(Appendix~\ref{app:engine-sensitivity}).
