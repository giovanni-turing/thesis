\section*{1. Final simulation results}

\paragraph{Premise (the signal gap is real).} Request rate misrepresents
vision demand: constant-fraction explains $R^2{=}0.12$ of the image rate
vs.\ $0.65$ of decode demand; image arrivals are an order of magnitude
burstier; the two signals' extreme minutes are essentially disjoint. In
simulation the pools' saturation knees sit an order of magnitude apart,
and inside the burst window the encoder pool misses its tail bound
\emph{at the trace's own recorded rate} --- invisible to rate/utilization
monitors.

\paragraph{Forecastability.} Zero-shot time-series foundation models beat
naive/EWMA/Holt-Winters in \textbf{20/20} cells; the vision signal is the
most persistent (autocorrelation half-life 69 min vs.\ 9 for requests);
the p90 band edge is empirically calibrated (coverage 0.88--0.90), holding
across the 12-step horizon and inside burst windows.

\paragraph{Closed loop --- the headline.} Over the 5.60-day,
749{,}601-request evaluation span, the p90-forecast schedule holds the
encoder tail bound at \textbf{0.62$\times$} the deployable static fleet's
provisioned worker-time (\textbf{669.7 vs.\ 1{,}074.8} worker-hours).
Forecast and reactive delay quantiles coincide through p99 over the span;
the latency advantage lives in the one-minute onsets. Against reactive
with warm floors (a reactive-favorable post-hoc sweep): the cheapest
evaluation-passing floor costs \textbf{1.20$\times$} the forecast's
worker-time under the readiness model most favorable to reactive, and
\textbf{1.67$\times$} under shipped re-warm mechanics. A
perfect-information reference at 0.292$\times$ marks remaining
signal-quality headroom.

\paragraph{Language pool (secondary).} The forecast-driven language policy
holds its SLA only through tail-aware capacity conversion; at its
published operating point it is near break-even vs.\ static
(0.965$\times$ time-matched, 1.03$\times$ request-matched). Week-scale
language grading: the reactive span TTFT p99 breaches
(\SI{1196.6}{\milli\second} vs.\ forecast \SI{651.1}{\milli\second},
consistent across three forecast models) with 37.4\% less total delay
mass for forecast.

\paragraph{Live-stack check.} Both burst-window verdicts reproduce in both
directions through Dynamo's \emph{real} control plane (mocked engines):
forecast passes (695 vs.\ 651 ms offline), reactive fails (3{,}531 vs.\
3{,}495 ms).

\section*{2. Physical validation (the plus --- new since we spoke)}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cost model on silicon:} encoder costs collected on RTX~5090
  (locked clocks; dataset submitted upstream to NVIDIA aiconfigurator as a
  draft PR) and validated against the serving engine's \emph{own} vision
  tower: within \textbf{12.8\%} across the entire $N\le64$ batching
  regime (attention operator within ${\approx}3\%$). Re-running the loop
  with measured costs moves the headline p99 only 589$\to$606 ms.
\item \textbf{Controller dispatches replayed on silicon} (five predeclared
  reps/arm, exact frozen dispatch, membership-exact): on the fastest-onset
  burst the simulated verdict survives with a ${\approx}29\times$ margin
  --- forecast $782.455\pm0.270$ ms (inside the \SI{1}{\second} bound)
  vs.\ floorless reactive $22{,}565.3\pm0.9$ ms. Every repetition
  reconstructs the banked simulation percentiles exactly before retiming.
\item \textbf{Opposite regime too:} on a calm 24-hour slice the
  simulation's structure is preserved on silicon --- static lowest,
  forecast/reactive near-tied, and every arm passes under the serving
  engine's measured service times. (A slower HuggingFace-eager stack
  inflates absolutes 1.5--1.6$\times$; reported as an engine sensitivity
  case, not a bound.)
\item \textbf{Integrity:} an 18-probe negative-gate suite (corrupted
  membership, reordered dispatch, changed fingerprints must fail loudly)
  passes from a clean checkout; all raw evidence is pinned at immutable
  commits with model/container hashes.
\end{itemize}
